A correct GST invoice protects you and your customer. It is the record your customer needs to claim input tax credit, and the document your accountant relies on when filing returns. Good billing software fills most of these details automatically, but it helps to know what a valid invoice must contain.

> This guide is general information, not tax advice. GST rules change; confirm the current requirements with your CA.

## What a GST tax invoice must include

Under Rule 46 of the CGST Rules, a tax invoice issued by a registered supplier should show:

1. Supplier's name, address and GSTIN
2. A consecutive invoice number, up to 16 characters, unique for the financial year
3. Date of issue
4. Customer's name, address and GSTIN, if the customer is registered
5. HSN code for goods (or SAC for services)
6. Description of the goods or services
7. Quantity and unit
8. Total value and taxable value, after any discount
9. Tax rate and tax amount, shown separately for CGST, SGST/UTGST or IGST, and cess if any
10. Place of supply, with the state name, for inter-state supplies
11. Whether tax is payable on reverse charge
12. Signature or digital signature of the supplier

For an unregistered customer with a taxable value of ₹50,000 or more, the invoice should also carry the customer's name and address and the delivery address, with the state name and code.

## CGST + SGST or IGST?

- **Same state** (intra-state supply): split the tax equally into CGST and SGST (or UTGST in a union territory). A 5% item is billed as 2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST.
- **Different state** (inter-state supply): charge the full rate as IGST.

Billing software should pick the right split from the customer's state, so you don't have to work it out at the counter.

## HSN code digits

Since April 2021:

- Aggregate turnover **up to ₹5 crore**: at least 4-digit HSN on B2B invoices
- Aggregate turnover **above ₹5 crore**: 6-digit HSN on all invoices

Saving the HSN code against each item once means it appears on every bill automatically.

## Bill of supply instead of a tax invoice

If you are registered under the **composition scheme**, or you sell only exempt goods, you issue a **bill of supply**, not a tax invoice, and you do not charge GST on it. Composition dealers also need to state that they are a composition taxable person not eligible to collect tax.

## E-invoicing and invoice timing

> This guide is general information, not tax advice. GST rules change; confirm the current requirements with your CA.

**E-invoicing threshold.** Since 1 August 2023, a business whose aggregate annual turnover has crossed **₹5 crore** in any financial year from 2017-18 onwards must generate e-invoices for B2B sales. Each such invoice is reported to the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP), which returns an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and a QR code to print on the bill. Sales to ordinary consumers (B2C) don't need e-invoices.

**When to issue the invoice.** For goods, the tax invoice is issued at or before the time the goods are removed or delivered. For services, it must be issued within **30 days** of supplying the service (45 days for banks and similar financial companies).

**30-day reporting window for e-invoices.** Larger businesses must also report each e-invoice to the IRP within 30 days of its invoice date, or the IRP won't accept it. This applied from November 2023 to businesses with turnover of ₹100 crore or more, and from April 2025 to those with ₹10 crore or more.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Gaps or repeats in the invoice number sequence
- Charging IGST on a local sale, or CGST + SGST on an inter-state one
- Missing the customer's GSTIN on a B2B bill, which blocks their input tax credit
- Rounding tax per line in a way that doesn't add up to the total
